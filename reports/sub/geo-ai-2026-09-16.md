## 2. Entity-consistency bug (Organization.description) — assessed as P0 for GEO

Per `reports/sub/technical-seo-2026-09-16.md` §6 Bug 1: every page's `Organization` JSON-LD ships the **identical, byte-for-byte** `description` field:

> "BiXBiT immersion cooling systems for mining ✅ Safe construction, maximum hashrate without a risk of overheating ✅ Non-toxic and eco-friendly coolant ➤ Worldwide shipping!"

This is cooling-product ad copy (complete with checkmark emoji and a CTA-style exclamation), not a company description, and it is repeated on the homepage, `/en/ams`, `/en/firmwares`, `/en/products/pdu`, `/en/faq`, `/en/documentation`, `/en/blog`, `/en/calc` — i.e. on pages that have nothing to do with cooling.

**GEO severity assessment: P0, not P1.** Reasoning:

1. **`Organization` schema is the single structured signal an AI crawler/retrieval system has for "what is this company"** — it's the machine-readable equivalent of the entity-definition sentence the June audit already flagged as P0-missing. A wrong answer here is strictly worse than no answer: it actively teaches extraction systems (Google Knowledge Graph, AI Overviews, any LLM-side knowledge-graph builder that ingests schema.org data) that BiXBiT = an immersion cooling coolant brand, full stop — erasing firmware, AMS, and PDU from the entity definition everywhere schema is consulted instead of body copy.
2. It directly contradicts the correct, differentiated body copy that (per `content/page-optimize/*.md` drafts) is being written for `/en/ams`, `/en/firmwares`, homepage — creating an **on-page-text vs. structured-data mismatch**. LLMs and Google's entity systems weigh consistency between visible content and markup as a trust/confidence signal (this is explicitly called out in the homepage draft's own §8.4 llms.txt note). Right now the site actively fails that consistency check on every page.
3. It is sitewide and mechanical (one template field), meaning the blast radius is the entire domain, not one page — and the fix is a one-line JSON field replacement, i.e. trivially low effort for a high-impact, sitewide correction. High-impact + low-effort is close to the canonical definition of P0 in this project's own impact/effort prioritization rule (CLAUDE.md §7.3).
4. Counter-consideration (why not P1): it is not currently blocking crawl/indexation and firmware/cooling pages still have correct body-text descriptions readable by LLM passage extraction that ignores schema, so it's not a total blackout of the correct entity story — just a corrupted structured-data channel running in parallel with a mostly-correct text channel. That nuance is why it's "P0 for GEO specifically" rather than "P0 site-emergency," but within this agent's GEO remit it should be treated with the same urgency as the missing llms.txt.

**Fix (already correctly scoped by technical-seo, restated here for the GEO priority list):** replace `description` only, keep `name`/`url`/`logo`/`sameAs` as-is:
```json
"description": "BiXBiT is an industrial provider of custom ASIC firmware, monitoring software, and cooling infrastructure for professional Bitcoin mining operations."
```
`[VERIFY: exact company description wording — needs content-owner sign-off, not to be shipped as generated copy without review, per CLAUDE.md rule #2]`. Once approved, this same sentence should also seed the entity-definition opening paragraphs already drafted in `content/page-optimize/homepage.md` §4 and the `llms.txt` rewrite (§1 above) — one canonical sentence, reused everywhere, is the actual GEO fix; three inconsistent versions across schema/body-copy/llms.txt would just relocate the consistency problem rather than solve it.

## 3. Blog (~400 posts) and Documentation — passage-citability assessment

Both sections are newly confirmed-real since June (previously undocumented / assumed not to exist). Sampled 2 recent blog posts (newest, 2026-09-09 and 2026-07-24 lastmod) and 1 documentation article live via WebFetch. Findings are qualitative, based only on these samples — not a full-corpus audit.

### Blog — `bixbit-at-wdms-bitmain-2026-next-step-for-mining-infrastructure` (newest post, 2026-09-09)
- Opens with an event/product narrative ("During the BITMAIN New Product Launch... BITMAIN presented the Antminer S23 XP Hyd...") — **brand name "BiXBiT" does not appear in the opening paragraph**, same structural gap the June audit flagged on the firmware pages.
- Concrete, citable facts are present: "energy efficiency below 9 J/TH", inlet-coolant temperature ranges "~20–50°C" and "~20–35°C", an efficiency-evolution figure "from 2,000 J/T to 11 J/T". These are third-party (Bitmain) product specs the post is reporting on, not BiXBiT's own specs — useful for topical relevance/authority signaling but not brand-attributable facts an LLM would cite as "BiXBiT says X."
- Spec data is **embedded as images** ("Таблица Antminer_EN.png") rather than structured text/HTML tables — **not machine-extractable** by passage-retrieval systems or AI crawlers, which read text, not image content (no evidence of alt-text/OCR-friendly captions checked, but images-as-tables is a real extraction blocker regardless).
- No FAQ, no Q&A structure, no definition-list.
- **Reads as event/news journalism, not a citable reference answer.**

### Blog — `hydro-asic-2026-ranking-models-for-largescale-mining-farms` (2026-07-24)
- Opens with a genuinely strong, generic-informational first sentence: "In 2026, choosing an ASIC for mining still starts not with hashrate, but with energy efficiency. For the B2B segment, the J/TH metric matters most..." — this is exactly the kind of direct-answer, definition-style opening that extracts well as a passage. But **again, brand absent from the opening** — BiXBiT only appears in the conclusion/CTA, alongside firmware/AMS promotion.
- Concrete comparison data present in text (not just images this time, per the fetch): "1,160 TH/s, 9.5 J/TH, and 11,020 W" for one model, similar figures for two others — this is real spec-table-shaped content, good for extraction if it's actually rendered as text/table markup rather than prose-embedded numbers (not independently confirmed via raw HTML this pass).
- No FAQ section.
- **Moderately high citability for the topical/generic Q ("what's an efficient hydro ASIC in 2026") but zero brand-attribution value** — an LLM could lift the spec comparison and cite Bitmain/MicroBT model data without ever needing to mention BiXBiT, since BiXBiT only shows up as a promotional afterthought, not as the source of the comparative claim.

### Documentation — `error-codes-for-whatsminer-asic`
- The hub page itself is a bare navigation shell with no body content — the actual error-code list lives one level deeper at `/list-of-errors-and-solutions-1` (not fetched this pass; flag for a follow-up check before drawing conclusions about that page's citability).
- "BiXBiT" is clearly and repeatedly attributed as the documentation provider in headers/title/footer — entity-attribution is solid here, better than the blog samples.
- No visible FAQ-schema-style Q&A on the hub page; error-code references are inherently well-suited to structured extraction (they're literally a lookup table by nature) **if** the child page renders them as real text/table content rather than images — unverified this pass.

### Net assessment
The Blog and Documentation sections are **real, actively maintained SEO/topical-authority content (~400 posts, weekly-ish cadence, newest 1 week old at audit time), but not built with AI-citability/passage-extraction in mind**: openings bury or omit the brand, spec data is sometimes image-embedded, and there is no FAQ/schema layer at the article level (confirmed no `Article`/`BlogPosting`/`FAQPage` JSON-LD was checked on blog templates by the technical-seo pass either — worth a follow-up specifically on blog templates, out of scope for the 10-page spot-check done today). This is a real opportunity, not a dead end: the content exists and covers relevant topics (hydro ASIC efficiency, immersion cooling, mining infrastructure trends) that overlap with the exact B2B queries this agent's June prompt-test matrix used — but today it functions as classic SEO/content-marketing copy, not GEO-shaped passage content.

- **Live check, 2026-09-16:** `https://bixbit.io/robots.txt` fetched — content is **byte-for-byte unchanged from June** (confirmed independently by both this session's WebFetch and the same-day technical-seo pass). No `GPTBot`, `ClaudeBot`, `PerplexityBot`, `Google-Extended`, or `ai-train` directives present. Only the original wildcard `User-agent: *` block with the same Disallow list (`/igra/`, `/assets/`, search, registration, tags, `/auth/`, `/download/`).
- **Net effect is unchanged and still acceptable, but not "done":** all AI crawlers remain implicitly allowed via the wildcard (no Disallow rule matches their paths), so there is **no active blockage** — but the dev-task-p0.md proposal was never deployed. This is a P2, not P0/P1: explicit allow-listing is a clarity/best-practice improvement, not a fix for an actual block. Correctly left lower-priority behind the llms.txt and entity-description fixes.
- `/download/` remains disallowed, still `[VERIFY]` for 3 months running (both June reports and today's technical-seo pass all flag it, none have confirmed whether firmware binaries live there) — relevant to GEO because if release notes/changelogs are served from `/download/`, AI crawlers can't index them either.
